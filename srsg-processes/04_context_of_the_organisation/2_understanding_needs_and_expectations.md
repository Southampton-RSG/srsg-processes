# 4.2: Understanding the Needs and Expectations of Interested Parties

## Who are our customers?

Here we use the term "Customer" to refer to someone who is paying/providing resources, who is working with us day-to-day, or who is the beneficiary of the work.  One person might be all three, but in larger projects they are likely to be separate. 

Often we see our customers as collaborators when they are researchers we are working with on a research software project. 

The Research Software Group (RSG) works with a range of internal and external customers:

- Researchers — the primary users of our software and training services
- Research Software Engineers — colleagues who engage with our community and training
- Doctoral training centres and CHEP — recipients of our training provision
- Research centres — collaborative partners within the university
- Funding bodies — organisations that commission or fund our work
- External organisations — companies engaging us for CPD or commercial projects (e.g. AstraZeneca)

## Who are our stakeholders?

Stakeholders have an interest in our work but are not direct recipients of our services:

- University senior management (VCs and Directors)
- Professional societies (e.g. SocRSE, SSI)
- Government bodies
- Other universities

## Understanding the requirements of interested parties

The RSG is committed to understanding and meeting the needs of our customers. We will:

- Establish clear requirements at the outset of every project or engagement, documented and agreed with the collaborator (see Requirements Process TEC.10).
- Maintain regular communication with collaborators throughout a project, using agreed channels and schedules.
- Actively seek feedback from customers on our work and use it to drive continuous improvement (see Feedback Procedure)
- Handle complaints promptly and fairly, escalating where necessary, and recording outcomes to inform future practice (see Complaints Procedure).
- Identify and manage risks proactively, both within projects and in collaborator relationships (see Risk Management Process ORG.8 - separate doc not yet created).
