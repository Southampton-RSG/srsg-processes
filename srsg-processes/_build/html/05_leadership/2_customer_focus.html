5.2: Customer Focus#

It is very important to the Group’s operation that our customers and stakeholders are happy with our work.

The purpose of this policy is to establish and manage a positive relationship with the customers by understanding their needs, objectives and expectations.

The RSG is committed to understanding and meeting the needs of our customers. We will:

  1. Establish clear requirements at the outset of every project or engagement, documented and agreed with the collaborator (see Requirements Process TEC.10).

  2. Maintain regular communication with collaborators throughout a project, using agreed channels and schedules.

  3. Actively seek feedback from customers on our work and use it to drive continuous improvement (see Feedback Procedure)

  4. Handle complaints promptly and fairly, escalating where necessary, and recording outcomes to inform future practice (see Complaints Procedure).

  5. Identify and manage risks proactively, both within projects and in collaborator relationships (see Risk Management Process ORG.8).

Customer Feedback Procedure#

Collecting feedback is important to understanding whether we have met the customer’s requirements. We collect feedback during the project and at the end of the project. The scale of feedback collection is dependent on the scale of the project - for a smaller software project (in the range of days to weeks), this might involve simply checking in verbally with the customer during a meeting to; for a larger software project, training course or event, this might involve a more formal feedback method such as a feedback survey. Usually the staff member working on the project will determine the most appropriate method of feedback collection for the project themselves, with guidance from their line manager if required.

Feedback could be collected as:

  • Verbal feedback during Sprint Review meetings

    • Suggested prompts include: Are communication frequency and format working for you? Is there anything you would like us to do differently?

  • Testimonials at the end of the project

    • A short overview of the collaborator’s view on the project. They are largely used for promotional purposes.

  • A feedback survey

    • For training projects, see the Training Guidance (document to be produced) for specific guidance and templates

Saving Feedback#

Feedback should be saved in a project folder with a link to the data copied in the “Project Summary” section of the project’s entry in RSG admin. Sensitive data should not be stored outside of the University’s firewall, e.g. not in Google Drive or GitHub.

Analysing Feedback#

Create a general summary of the feedback, including any notably positive or negative feedback themes. Also note any recurring issues or requests. For analysing feedback on training workshops, see the Training Guidance (document to be produced).

Acting on Feedback#

  1. Any significant feedback should be raised promptly with the line manager so that changes to the project can be agreed.

  2. At the end of the project, the staff member should gather feedback collected during the project and at the end of the project and share a summary with their line manager.

  3. If systemic issues are identified, agree updates with line manager to processes and relevant materials (training content, project templates, etc).

  4. Risks identified through feedback should be added to the common risk list for future projects.

  5. After each large project, conduct a lessons learned meeting with at least one other SRSG staff member to reflect on what went well and what could be improved.

  6. If appropriate, share a summary of the feedback to the wider group so others can learn from it.

Issue Management Procedure#

Here we refer to interpersonal issues rather than GitHub issues.

The project initiation document provides guidance for the customer on how to raise an issue.

If a customer raises an issue to any member of staff, they must inform their line manager promptly. Responses to issues should always represent the SRSG as a group, not an individual staff member.

If a member of staff would like to raise an issue about the customer, they should talk to their line manager in the first instance.

Smaller issues will be resolved through discussion between the line manager, staff member and customer. More serious issues will be addressed according to the University of Southampton’s Grievance Procedure.

All communication about customers should be respectful, even if the communication is not sent to them. This is because customers have the right to submit a Subject Access Request for any information we hold on them, which would include our communications about them.

Handling an Issue#

  1. The issue should be acknowledged as soon as possible.

  2. The line manager talks with the relevant staff member and customer to understand the complaint.

  3. Decide whether the issue needs to be escalated to the University’s Grievance Procedure. If escalation is required, follow the processes set out in the University’s Grievance Procedure. Otherwise, the line manager responds to the collaborator with a proposed resolution. Where possible, this should include what will change as a result of the issue being raised.

  4. All complaints and their outcomes are recorded by the line manager, typically in RSG Admin. Provision for storing serious complaints will be dealt with individually.

  5. If the collaborator is not satisfied with the initial response, the complaint is escalated to the Director level.

  6. Add any newly identified risks to the common risk list and update the Risk Register.

Best efforts should be made to continue work as normal throughout the issue resolution procedure.
